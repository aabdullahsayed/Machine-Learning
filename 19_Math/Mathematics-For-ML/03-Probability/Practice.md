# Practice — Probability

1. Simulate flipping a fair coin 1000 times in Python, and confirm the empirical probability of heads is close to 0.5.
2. Implement Bayes' theorem from scratch to solve the "medical test" example in `Bayes-Theorem.md` — verify you get ≈50%.
3. Generate 10,000 samples from a Gaussian distribution (`np.random.normal`), plot a histogram, and compute the sample mean/variance — compare to the true `μ`, `σ²` you used to generate it.
4. Build a simple Naive Bayes spam classifier from scratch (bag-of-words + Bayes' theorem) on a small toy dataset of "spam"/"not spam" example sentences.
5. Implement Xavier initialization from scratch for a weight matrix, and verify empirically (by checking output variance) that it keeps activations at a stable scale compared to naive `N(0,1)` initialization.
6. Explain in your own words: why is cross-entropy loss connected to comparing two probability distributions, rather than just being an arbitrary formula?
