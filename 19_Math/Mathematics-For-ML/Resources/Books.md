# Recommended Books & Courses

## Foundational math (beginner-friendly)
- **"Mathematics for Machine Learning"** by Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong — free online (https://mml-book.github.io/) — covers exactly the topics in this repo (linear algebra, calculus, probability), written specifically for ML.
- **3Blue1Brown's "Essence of Linear Algebra" and "Essence of Calculus"** (YouTube) — the best visual/geometric intuition builder for vectors, matrices, eigenvectors, derivatives, and the gradient. Watch before or alongside `01-Linear-Algebra` and `02-Calculus`.

## Probability & Statistics
- **"Introduction to Probability"** by Blitzstein & Hwang — rigorous but readable, free lecture videos available (Harvard Stat 110).
- **"All of Statistics"** by Larry Wasserman — a dense but excellent single-book overview connecting probability, statistics, and their use in modern data science/ML.

## ML-specific math and theory
- **"The Elements of Statistical Learning"** by Hastie, Tibshirani, Friedman — free online, the classic rigorous reference for the statistical foundations of ML methods (regression, regularization, trees, SVMs).
- **"Deep Learning"** by Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville — free online (https://www.deeplearningbook.org/), Part I covers exactly the math (linear algebra, probability, numerical computation) needed for the rest of the book's deep learning content.
- **"Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning"** by Christopher Bishop — a classic, thorough treatment of the probabilistic/Bayesian view of ML.

## Optimization
- **"Convex Optimization"** by Boyd & Vandenberghe — free online (https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/), the definitive reference on convexity, Lagrange multipliers, and KKT conditions.

## Courses
- **Stanford CS229 (Machine Learning)** — lecture notes cover the math derivations (MLE, gradient descent, etc.) behind classical ML algorithms in detail, freely available online.
- **Andrew Ng's Deep Learning Specialization (Coursera)** — builds intuition for backpropagation, optimization, and regularization with a practical, code-first approach.
- **MIT 18.06 (Linear Algebra)** by Gilbert Strang — free OpenCourseWare lectures, widely considered one of the best linear algebra courses available anywhere.
