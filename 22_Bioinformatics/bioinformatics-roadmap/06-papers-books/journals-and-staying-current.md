# Journals & Staying Current

## Core Bioinformatics/Computational Journals

- **Bioinformatics** (Oxford) — the flagship methods journal for the field
- **Genome Research** / **Genome Biology** — genomics-focused, high impact
- **Nature Methods** / **Nature Biotechnology** — broader but frequently
  publishes major computational methods (AlphaFold, Seurat, etc. all
  appeared in Nature-family journals)
- **PLOS Computational Biology** — open access, broad computational
  biology scope
- **Nucleic Acids Research (NAR)** — publishes many widely-used
  databases and tools (also runs the annual "Web Server" and "Database"
  special issues, useful for finding tools)
- **BMC Bioinformatics** — open access, high volume, good for tool papers

## Preprint Servers

- **bioRxiv** — the primary preprint server for biology/bioinformatics;
  check weekly in your subfield
- **arXiv (q-bio section)** — more computational/quantitative biology,
  overlaps with bioRxiv

## Conferences (also publish proceedings worth reading)

- **ISMB/ECCB** (Intelligent Systems for Molecular Biology) — the
  premier bioinformatics conference
- **RECOMB** (Research in Computational Molecular Biology) — more
  algorithm/theory-focused
- **NeurIPS/ICML workshops** on computational biology — where ML-bio
  crossover work often appears first

## Practical Habit

Set up a weekly routine (even 30 minutes):
1. Skim bioRxiv's bioinformatics/genomics category new submissions
2. Follow 5–10 researchers in your chosen track on Twitter/X or Bluesky
   — much bioinformatics discourse happens there
3. Use a reference manager (Zotero is free and good) from day one —
   tag papers by track/method so your reading compounds into a
   searchable personal literature base
