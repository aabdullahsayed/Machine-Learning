# Recommended Textbooks

Organized by phase. "Priority" books are worth owning/reading cover to
cover; others are references to dip into as needed.

## Foundations (Biology for CS people)

- **"Molecular Biology of the Cell"** — Alberts et al. — the definitive
  molecular biology reference. Dense; use selectively (chapters on DNA,
  gene expression, and central dogma) rather than cover-to-cover.
- **"Molecular Biology of the Gene"** — Watson et al. — more genetics-
  and mechanism-focused, a common alternative/companion to Alberts.

## Core Bioinformatics (Priority)

- **"Bioinformatics Algorithms: An Active Learning Approach"** —
  Compeau & Pevzner — ⭐ the single best book for a CS student entering
  this field. Teaches algorithms (DP, graphs, HMMs) through biological
  problems, pairs directly with the Rosalind platform (same authors
  built Rosalind). Start here for Phase 3.
- **"Biological Sequence Analysis: Probabilistic Models of Proteins and
  Nucleic Acids"** — Durbin, Eddy, Krogh, Mitchison — the classic
  reference on HMMs and probabilistic sequence models; more formal/
  mathematical than Compeau & Pevzner, good second pass.
- **"An Introduction to Bioinformatics Algorithms"** — Jones & Pevzner —
  earlier edition/companion text, still useful as a reference.

## Statistics & Genomics Data Analysis

- **"Statistical Methods in Bioinformatics"** — Ewens & Grant — solid
  statistics grounding specific to sequence/genomic data.
- **"Modern Statistics for Modern Biology"** — Susan Holmes & Wolfgang
  Huber — free online (web version), excellent, R-based, directly
  covers RNA-seq/multiple testing/clustering as in Phase 4.
  (https://www.huber.embl.de/msmb/)
- **"Computational Genome Analysis: An Introduction"** — Deonier,
  Tavaré, Waterman — good bridge between algorithms and statistics.

## Machine Learning / Deep Learning in Biology

- **"Deep Learning for the Life Sciences"** — Ramsundar, Eastman,
  Walters, Pande — practical, code-oriented, covers molecules, genomics,
  microscopy.
- **"Machine Learning in Bioinformatics"** sections of general ML texts
  (e.g., Bishop's PRML for theory) — supplement with domain-specific
  papers rather than a single ML-bio textbook, since this area moves
  fast and papers are more current than any textbook.

## Structural Bioinformatics

- **"Introduction to Protein Structure"** — Branden & Tooze — classic,
  approachable intro to protein structure for non-structural-biologists.
- **"Structural Bioinformatics"** — Gu & Bourne (eds.) — comprehensive
  reference, more advanced.

## Genomics/Population Genetics

- **"An Introduction to Population Genetics: Theory and Applications"**
  — Rasmus Nielsen & Montgomery Slatkin.
- **"Principles of Population Genetics"** — Hartl & Clark — the standard
  academic reference.

## General/Reference

- **"Bioinformatics Data Skills"** — Vince Buffalo — ⭐ excellent
  practical companion for Phase 2 (Unix, reproducibility, real pipelines,
  version control) — arguably the most CS-friendly bioinformatics book
  in terms of software engineering practices.

## Free Online Resources Worth Treating Like Textbooks

- Rosalind.info (problem sets, pairs with Compeau & Pevzner)
- NCBI Bookshelf (free hosted biology/genetics textbooks)
- "Modern Statistics for Modern Biology" (linked above, fully free)
- Software Carpentry / Data Carpentry lessons (practical skills)
